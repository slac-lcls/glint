Recorded replay — not a live beamline, and not a simulation. Two real datasets, one stream: the cxidb-17 lysozyme frames (454 of the 480-frame extension, CXIDB entry 17, CC0) and the cxidb-45 Proteinase K frames (446 of 907, CXIDB entry 45, CC0), interleaved by a seeded schedule (experiments/schedules/lyso_prok_switch.json, seed 7: 8 scenes, frames taken in file order, none reused). The interleaving is the one planted thing on this page, and it is drawn as the ground truth it is: the schedule panel and the ribbon under the composition chart. The peaks are real, the cells are recovered blind, the order is scripted.

Where the peaks come from. Lysozyme: CrystFEL 0.12.0 peakfinder8 peaks read out of the blind indexamajig stream for the 480 images and converted to q through the package's own bridge at the published fixed wavelength (1.322216 Å). Proteinase K: the deposit's reciprocal-lattice-point lists, the 170 brightest peaks per frame — the same lists DIALS was given in the paper's head-to-head, so the cap is part of the record, not a choice made here. No pixels are replayed, no peak finder runs, and nothing is integrated: the driver is fed q-vectors and every frame is index-only.

The indexer is the shipped glint.stream_driver.StreamDriver at commit bd79038e77d4, cold-started with no cell (Mc=None), at the published 480 arm — B=20, dmin=2.0, tol=0.002, warmup_nbest=3, min_inliers=10, min_inlier_frac=0.15, warmup_rescue, adaptive_relock — plus rescue_buffer=64 (the retroactive index-only rescue the re-lock uses) and a naming roster of the two reference cells. The roster names a cell after the driver finds it; it never hands the indexer a cell. Events on (events=True) — every record on this page is one of the driver's own per-frame outcomes. OFF: retry cascade, geometry refinement, second-lattice detection, alias gate, lock probe.

What happened. Consensus locked the first cell after 5 frames and the registry named it Lysozyme; the 4 frames spent on discovery were re-indexed against it. When the schedule started blending Proteinase K in, its frames missed, the watchdog's misses voted a recurring cell the active set did not explain, and at frame 145 the driver added a second cell — named Proteinase K from the roster — and re-indexed the 10 misses still in its buffer against it. One re-lock, no alias. Over the run the watchdog rescued 15 individual frames the batch pass had missed. Final active set: 2 cells; Lysozyme 512 frames; Proteinase K 315 frames.

Scored honestly. Each frame is scored at the paper's strict bar (≥25% of spots and ≥10 reflections) against its own species' reference lattice, so a frame claimed by the wrong cell scores zero. The run indexes 582/900: lysozyme 309/454, Proteinase K 273/446. Attribution, planted species → cell: lysozyme frames → {'lyso': 427, '-': 19, 'prok': 8}; Proteinase K frames → {'lyso': 85, 'prok': 307, '-': 54} (- = missed).

Solo controls on the same frames. Lysozyme 311/454 alone (gate480.json) vs 309/454 in the mixture; Proteinase K 375/446 alone (prok907.json) vs 273/446 in the mixture. Lysozyme pays a couple of frames for the mixture. Proteinase K pays about a hundred, and most of that is one mechanism: 85 ProK frames were claimed first by the lysozyme cell — the first-fit cascade tries cell 0 first and a dense 170-peak frame gives it enough chance near-integer hits to pass the live gate — and none of them pass the strict bar under that cell. That is the measured case for best-fit assignment; this run does not do it, and the page shows the cost rather than hiding it.

Recorded on NVIDIA A100-SXM4-40GB (sdfampere036), torch 2.1.0.post302, numpy 1.26.3; 4.1 s of wall time for the whole replay (900 frames). Playback is looped and the frame rate is a display choice; every counter, both cells, the lock and re-lock frames, the ring occupancy, the rescues and the attribution are read out of that one recorded run. Cells are printed as measured (medians over the frames the driver attributed to them) and are not re-jittered for the animation.
